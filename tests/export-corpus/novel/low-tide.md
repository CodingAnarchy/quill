By morning the boat had gone, and the tide had pulled back so far that the harbor looked like a room with the furniture taken out.

Mara walked the wet sand in her father's boots. They were *too big*, and she had stuffed the toes with newspaper, and every step made a small, private sound.

She sang the keeper's song under her breath, the one he hummed over the lamp:

```verse common meter / abcb
The light is lit, the oil is in,
  the wick is trimmed and true;
and every ship that passes by
  is one I never knew.

The tide goes out, the tide comes *in*,
  the gulls go round and round;
```

* * *

The ledger was the first thing she checked. He kept it on the shelf above the stove: every ship that passed, every hour the light burned, every barrel of oil. It was not on the shelf.

**It was not anywhere.**
